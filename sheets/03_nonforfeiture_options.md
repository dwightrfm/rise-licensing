# Nonforfeiture Options: What Happens to the Cash Value When You Stop Paying

Audience: people studying for the life insurance licensing exam.
Goal: the viewer knows all three nonforfeiture options and can tell extended term from reduced paid-up without guessing.

## Why this matters
This is one of the most missed topics on the life exam. Students mix up extended term and reduced paid-up every time. By the end of this you will not.

## What "nonforfeiture" means
Forfeit means to lose something. NONforfeiture means you do NOT lose it.
Permanent life insurance (like whole life) builds cash value over time. If the policyowner stops paying premiums, state law says they cannot just lose that cash value. They get to choose what to do with it.
Nonforfeiture options apply only to policies that have cash value. Term insurance has no cash value, so it has no nonforfeiture options.

## Option 1: Cash surrender
Take the cash value as money and walk away. The coverage ends.
The policyowner gets the cash surrender value (cash value minus any loans and surrender charges).
Tax note: if the cash received is more than the total premiums paid (the cost basis), the gain is taxable as ordinary income.
Picture: cashing in the piggy bank and closing the account.

## Option 2: Reduced paid-up insurance
Use the cash value as a single payment to buy a SMALLER policy of the SAME type (permanent) that is fully paid for life.
No more premiums ever. Coverage lasts for life. The death benefit is smaller.
Memory: Reduced = Smaller amount, for life.
The reduced paid-up policy keeps building cash value of its own.

## Option 3: Extended term insurance
Use the cash value as a single payment to buy TERM insurance for the SAME full face amount, for as long as the money lasts.
No more premiums. The death benefit stays the same. But coverage only lasts for a set period of time, then it ends.
Memory: Extended = Same amount, shorter time.
Extended term is usually the AUTOMATIC option. If the policyowner stops paying and does not pick an option, most policies default to extended term.

## The side by side
Reduced paid-up: amount goes DOWN, time stays for LIFE.
Extended term: amount stays the SAME, time goes DOWN to a set period.
Cash surrender: coverage ENDS, you take the money.

Picture: you have a fixed amount of gas money. Reduced paid-up buys a smaller car you can drive forever. Extended term rents the same big car, but only until the money runs out.

## A close cousin: automatic premium loan
Automatic premium loan is a policy provision, not one of the three nonforfeiture options. If a premium is missed, the insurer automatically borrows from the cash value to pay it, so the policy stays in force. The loan builds interest and reduces the death benefit if not paid back.

## Common exam traps
- "Term insurance offers nonforfeiture options." False. No cash value, no nonforfeiture options.
- "Extended term keeps the policy in force for life at a lower amount." False. That is reduced paid-up.
- "Reduced paid-up keeps the same face amount." False. That is extended term.
- "If the policyowner does not choose, the policy is surrendered for cash." False. The usual default is extended term.
- "Automatic premium loan is a nonforfeiture option." False. It is a separate provision.

## Quick check
1. Same death benefit, but only for a limited time. Which option? (Extended term)
2. Smaller death benefit, paid up for life. Which option? (Reduced paid-up)
3. Which option is usually the automatic default? (Extended term)
