# Risk, Perils and Hazards: The Words Insurance Is Built On

Audience: people studying for the life insurance licensing exam.
Goal: the viewer can define risk, tell pure risk from speculative risk, and tell a peril from a hazard.

## What is risk?
Risk is uncertainty about a loss. It is the chance that something bad happens and you lose money because of it.
Insurance exists to deal with that uncertainty.

## Pure risk vs speculative risk
Pure risk has only two outcomes: you lose, or nothing happens. There is no way to win. Example: dying early, getting sick, a house fire.
Speculative risk has three outcomes: lose, break even, or WIN. Example: buying a stock, gambling, starting a business.
Only pure risk is insurable. Insurance pays you back for a loss. It never helps you make a profit.
Picture: a house fire can only hurt you. A poker hand can hurt you or pay you. Insurance covers the fire, never the poker hand.

## Peril vs hazard
Peril is the CAUSE of a loss. What actually happened. Examples: death, illness, accident, fire.
Hazard is a CONDITION that makes a loss MORE LIKELY or worse. It is not the cause. It raises the odds.

Three types of hazard:
1. Physical hazard: a physical condition. Examples: high blood pressure, smoking, skydiving, a dangerous job.
2. Moral hazard: dishonesty or bad character. Example: someone who would lie on an application or fake a claim.
3. Morale hazard: carelessness or indifference BECAUSE the person has insurance. Example: "I'm covered, so I don't care how I drive."

## Memory trick
Peril = what happened. Hazard = what made it more likely.
Moral = a lie. Morale = a shrug.
Pure = lose or nothing. Speculative = lose, nothing, or win.
Buying insurance = transfer.

## Five ways to handle risk (remember SPRAT)
Share: split the risk with others (business partners, a family pooling money).
Prevent or reduce: lower the odds or the size of loss (quit smoking, wear a seatbelt).
Retain: keep the risk yourself (a high deductible, self-insuring a small loss).
Avoid: stay away from the activity entirely (never skydive).
Transfer: pay someone else to carry the risk. Buying insurance is TRANSFER.

## Law of large numbers
The bigger the group of similar people, the more accurately an insurer can predict how many losses will happen. An insurer cannot predict when ONE person will die, but it can predict how many out of 100,000 people the same age will die this year.

## Adverse selection
People who are more likely to have a loss are more likely to want insurance. Example: someone who just got a bad diagnosis rushes to buy coverage. Insurers use underwriting (health questions, exams, records) to protect against adverse selection.

## What makes a risk insurable
- The loss is due to chance, not on purpose.
- The loss is definite and measurable (you can tell when it happened and how much).
- There is a large number of similar exposures, so losses are predictable.
- The loss is not catastrophic for the insurer (not everyone at once).
- The premium is affordable compared with the possible loss.

## Common exam traps
- "Insurance covers speculative risk." False. Pure risk only.
- "Smoking is a peril." False. Smoking is a physical hazard. The peril is death or illness.
- "Morale hazard means the person is dishonest." False. That is moral hazard. Morale is indifference.
- "Buying insurance is risk retention." False. It is risk transfer.

## Quick check
1. A client drives carelessly because they have good coverage. Which hazard? (Morale)
2. Buying a lottery ticket is what kind of risk? (Speculative)
3. Buying life insurance is which risk method? (Transfer)
