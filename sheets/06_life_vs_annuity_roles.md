# Life Insurance vs Annuities: Same Roles, Opposite Jobs

Audience: people studying for the life insurance licensing exam.
Goal: the viewer understands that life insurance and annuities do opposite jobs, and can name who plays each role in each contract.

## The big idea
Life insurance protects against dying too SOON.
An annuity protects against living too LONG.

Life insurance CREATES an estate. One payment at death puts a pile of money there for the family.
An annuity LIQUIDATES an estate. It turns a pile of money into a stream of income you cannot outlive.

Picture: life insurance is a bucket that fills up all at once when someone dies. An annuity is a bucket with a faucet that drips income for as long as you live.

## What they have in common
Both are sold by insurance companies.
Both use mortality tables (how long people are expected to live).
Both can grow money tax-deferred inside the contract.
Both have an owner who controls the contract and a beneficiary.

The difference in how mortality works: in life insurance, the insurer loses money when someone dies EARLY. In an annuity, the insurer loses money when someone lives a LONG time.

## The roles in a life insurance policy
Policyowner: the person who controls the contract. They name and change the beneficiary, take loans, surrender it, and choose options. Owns it, even if they are not the one covered.
Insured: the person whose life is covered. When the insured dies, the death benefit is paid.
Payor: the person who pays the premium. Usually the owner, but not always.
Beneficiary: the person who receives the death benefit. A primary beneficiary is first in line. A contingent beneficiary gets paid only if the primary is no longer living.
Revocable beneficiary: the owner can change them anytime. Irrevocable beneficiary: the owner needs that beneficiary's written permission to change them or take certain actions.

Example: a mom (owner and payor) buys a policy on her husband (insured) and names their kids (beneficiaries). Three different people in four roles.

## The roles in an annuity
Owner (contract owner): controls the contract, puts the money in, picks the options, can withdraw or surrender.
Annuitant: the person whose life expectancy is used to figure the income payments. The annuitant is the annuity's version of the insured. It is the "measuring life." The annuitant must be a real person, not a company or trust.
Payor: whoever puts the money in, usually the owner.
Beneficiary: receives the value if death happens BEFORE income payments begin (during the accumulation phase). This death benefit is generally the greater of the money put in or the current account value.
Payee: whoever receives the income checks, usually the annuitant.

## The matching trick
Life insurance insured = Annuity annuitant. Both are the "measuring life."
Owner = Owner. Controls it in both.
Payor = Payor. Writes the checks in both.
Beneficiary = Beneficiary. Gets paid at death in both.

## The two phases of an annuity
Accumulation phase: money goes IN and grows tax-deferred.
Annuitization (payout) phase: money comes OUT as income.
Common payout choices: life only (highest check, stops at death), life with period certain (guaranteed for a minimum number of years), refund life (unused money goes back to a beneficiary), joint and survivor (covers two people), period certain (fixed number of years, not tied to life).

## Taxes in one line each
Life insurance death benefit: generally received income-tax-free by the beneficiary.
Annuity gains: taxed as ordinary income when they come out. Only the growth is taxed, not the money originally put in from after-tax dollars.

## Common exam traps
- "The annuitant must be the owner." False. They can be different people.
- "An annuity protects against dying too soon." False. That is life insurance. Annuities protect against living too long.
- "The insurer loses money on an annuity when the annuitant dies early." False. Early death in an annuity usually means the insurer pays out less. Long life costs the insurer.
- "The annuity beneficiary is paid after income payments begin under a life-only payout." False. Life only stops at the annuitant's death and leaves nothing behind.

## Quick check
1. In an annuity, whose life expectancy sets the payments? (The annuitant)
2. Which product creates an estate? (Life insurance)
3. Who can change a revocable beneficiary? (The policyowner)
