# Life Insurance Contract Basics: Unilateral, Adhesion, Aleatory, Conditional

Audience: people studying for the life insurance licensing exam.
Goal: by the end, the viewer can tell these four contract words apart in one sentence each.

## Why this matters
These words show up on almost every life licensing exam. Students mix them up because they all sound like legal jargon. Each one answers a different simple question.

## The four things every legal contract needs
1. Agreement: an offer and an acceptance. In life insurance, the applicant usually makes the offer by sending the application with the first premium. The insurer accepts by issuing the policy.
2. Consideration: something of value from each side. The applicant gives the statements on the application plus the first premium. The insurer gives its promise to pay.
3. Competent parties: both sides are legally able to sign (of legal age, sound mind).
4. Legal purpose: the contract cannot be for something illegal. Insurable interest is part of this.

## Unilateral contract
Question it answers: who is making a promise the courts can enforce?
Answer: only ONE side, the insurance company.
The policyowner promises nothing. They can stop paying any time and the policy just lapses. Nobody sues them.
The insurer IS bound. If premiums are paid and a covered death happens, it must pay.
Picture: a vending machine. The machine promises a snack if you put money in. You never promised to buy anything.

## Contract of adhesion
Question it answers: who wrote the contract?
Answer: the insurance company wrote every word. The buyer can only take it or leave it. No negotiating the wording.
Because the insurer wrote it, any unclear or ambiguous wording is interpreted in favor of the policyowner (the insured side), not the company.
Picture: the terms and conditions on a phone app. You click "accept" or you do not get the app.

## Aleatory contract
Question it answers: is the trade equal?
Answer: no. It is an unequal exchange that depends on an uncertain future event.
Someone may pay one month of premium and the insurer pays a large death benefit. Someone else pays for 30 years and the insurer pays nothing because the term ended.
Picture: a lottery ticket. Small amount in, possibly a big amount out, and chance decides.

## Conditional contract
Question it answers: does the insurer have to pay no matter what?
Answer: no. The insurer pays only IF certain conditions are met. Premiums must be paid. Proof of death must be submitted. The loss must be covered.
Picture: "If you clean your room, then you get your allowance."

## Two more terms that travel with these
Utmost good faith: both sides must be honest. The insurer relies on what the applicant says.
Representations vs warranties: on a life application, the applicant's answers are representations, meaning true to the best of their knowledge. A warranty is a statement guaranteed to be exactly true. A misrepresentation of a MATERIAL fact (one that would have changed the insurer's decision) can let the insurer void the contract during the contestable period.
Concealment: hiding a material fact on purpose.

## Insurable interest
For life insurance, insurable interest must exist when the policy is applied for. It does NOT have to exist at the time of death.
You have insurable interest in your own life, your spouse, close family who depend on you, a business partner, or a key employee. A creditor has insurable interest in a debtor up to the amount owed.

## Memory trick
Unilateral = ONE promise.
Adhesion = THEIR words, take it or leave it.
Aleatory = UNEQUAL, depends on chance.
Conditional = IF, THEN.

## Common exam traps
- "The policyowner is legally required to keep paying premiums." False. That is why it is unilateral.
- "Ambiguous wording is interpreted in favor of the insurer." False. Adhesion means it goes to the policyowner.
- "Insurable interest must exist at the time of death." False for life insurance. It must exist at application.

## Quick check
1. A company wrote the policy and the buyer could not change a word. Which term? (Adhesion)
2. Only the insurer made a legally enforceable promise. Which term? (Unilateral)
3. A $40 premium produced a $250,000 death claim. Which term describes this unequal trade? (Aleatory)
