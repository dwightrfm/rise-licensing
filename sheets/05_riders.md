# Life Insurance Riders: What Each One Does

Audience: people studying for the life insurance licensing exam.
Goal: the viewer can match each common rider to the one thing it does.

## What a rider is
A rider is an add-on to a life insurance policy. It changes or adds a benefit, usually for an extra cost. Think of it like toppings on a pizza. The policy is the pizza. Riders are the toppings.

The easy way to remember riders is to sort them by the question they answer.

## Group 1: Riders that stop the bills (premium waivers)

Waiver of premium
If the INSURED becomes totally disabled, the insurer pays the premiums for them while the disability lasts.
There is a waiting period, typically six months, before the waiver kicks in. Once approved, premiums paid during the waiting period are usually refunded.
It usually ends at a set age, such as 60 or 65.
Memory: "I got hurt, my bills stop."

Payor benefit rider
Used on a policy for a child (a juvenile policy). If the PAYOR, usually a parent, dies or becomes disabled, the premiums are waived until the child reaches a set age, often 21 or 25.
Memory: "Mom or Dad can't pay, the policy keeps going for the kid."

The difference: waiver of premium protects against disability of the INSURED. Payor benefit protects against death or disability of the PERSON PAYING.

## Group 2: Riders that pay MORE at death

Accidental death benefit (ADB), often called double indemnity
Pays an extra amount, often equal to the face amount, if the insured dies from an ACCIDENT. The death must usually happen within a set time of the accident, commonly 90 days.
Illness deaths do not trigger it. Coverage usually ends around age 65 or 70.

Accidental death and dismemberment (AD&D)
Pays the principal sum for accidental death, and a capital sum (part of the principal, often half) for losing a limb or eyesight in an accident.

Memory: Accidental riders pay extra only for accidents, never for illness.

## Group 3: Riders that let you buy MORE later

Guaranteed insurability rider (also called guaranteed purchase option)
Lets the policyowner buy more coverage at set ages or life events (like marriage or the birth of a child) WITHOUT proving they are still healthy.
The options usually stop around age 40.
Memory: "Lock in your health today, buy more later."

Cost of living rider
Raises the death benefit over time to keep up with inflation, usually tied to the Consumer Price Index.

## Group 4: Riders that add MORE PEOPLE

Term riders on a permanent policy
Spouse or other insured rider: adds term coverage on another person.
Children's rider: covers all children in the family under one rider, usually convertible to permanent coverage later without proof of health.
Family rider: covers the spouse and children together.

## Group 5: Riders that pay EARLY, while alive

Accelerated death benefit (also called living benefit)
If the insured is diagnosed with a terminal illness (and on many policies, a chronic or critical illness), part of the death benefit is paid early. Whatever is paid early is subtracted from what the beneficiary gets later.

Long-term care rider
Pays part of the death benefit early to cover long-term care costs, like a nursing home or home care.

## The quick map
Stop the bills: waiver of premium, payor benefit.
Pay more at death: accidental death, AD&D.
Buy more later: guaranteed insurability, cost of living.
Add more people: spouse, children's, family riders.
Pay early: accelerated death benefit, long-term care.

## Common exam traps
- "Accidental death benefit pays double if the insured dies of cancer." False. Accidents only.
- "Waiver of premium covers the payor's disability on a child's policy." False. That is payor benefit.
- "Guaranteed insurability requires a new medical exam." False. That is the whole point: no proof of insurability.
- "An accelerated benefit is paid on top of the full death benefit." False. It reduces the death benefit.

## Quick check
1. A dad paying for his daughter's policy dies. Which rider keeps it going? (Payor benefit)
2. The insured becomes totally disabled. Which rider pays the premium? (Waiver of premium)
3. A 28-year-old wants to add coverage after having a baby without a new exam. Which rider? (Guaranteed insurability)
