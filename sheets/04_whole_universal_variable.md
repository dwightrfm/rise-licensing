# Whole, Universal and Variable Life: Two Questions Sort Them All

Audience: people studying for the life insurance licensing exam.
Goal: the viewer can tell the three types apart by asking two questions: is the premium flexible, and who carries the investment risk?

## Why this matters
These three get confused constantly on the exam. Each one is built on whole life, then changes one thing.

## The two questions that sort everything
1. Is the premium fixed or flexible?
2. Who carries the investment risk, the insurance company or the policyowner?

## Whole life
Premium: fixed. Same amount every time, it never goes up.
Death benefit: guaranteed and level.
Cash value: guaranteed. It grows on a set schedule shown in the policy.
Investment risk: the INSURANCE COMPANY carries it. The money sits in the insurer's general account.
Coverage lasts for the insured's whole life. The policy endows (cash value equals the face amount) at a set maturity age, typically 100 or 121.
Versions you will see: straight (ordinary) life, where premiums are paid for life; limited pay, like 20-pay life or life paid up at 65, where premiums are higher but stop early; single premium, paid all at once.
Picture: a fixed-rate mortgage in reverse. Everything is set and guaranteed on day one.

## Universal life (UL)
Premium: FLEXIBLE. Within limits, the policyowner can pay more, pay less, or skip a payment, as long as there is enough cash value to cover the monthly cost of insurance.
Death benefit: adjustable. The owner can raise it (with proof of insurability) or lower it.
Two death benefit options:
- Option A (also called Option 1): level death benefit. The death benefit stays the face amount.
- Option B (also called Option 2): increasing death benefit. The death benefit equals the face amount PLUS the cash value.
Cash value: earns a current interest rate set by the insurer, with a guaranteed minimum rate. Interest above the minimum is not guaranteed.
Investment risk: shared. The insurer guarantees the minimum; the owner gets whatever is credited above it.
UL is "unbundled," meaning the cost of insurance, expenses and cash value are shown separately so the owner can see where the money goes.
Indexed universal life is a version of UL where interest credits are tied to a market index, usually with a floor (often zero) and a cap.
Memory: Universal = flexible.

## Variable life (VL)
Premium: FIXED, like whole life.
Cash value: NOT guaranteed. The money goes into the insurer's SEPARATE account and the owner picks subaccounts (similar to mutual funds). Cash value can go up or down with the market.
Death benefit: has a guaranteed minimum (the face amount). It can rise if investments do well.
Investment risk: the POLICYOWNER carries it.
Because it is a security, a person selling variable life needs a life insurance license AND a securities registration (FINRA Series 6 or Series 7), and the buyer must receive a prospectus.
Memory: Variable = investment risk on the owner.

## Variable universal life (VUL)
Combines both: FLEXIBLE premium like universal life, plus separate account investments like variable life. Owner carries the investment risk. Also a security, needs the securities registration.

## The side by side
Whole life: fixed premium, guaranteed cash value, insurer carries risk, general account.
Universal life: flexible premium, minimum guaranteed interest, adjustable death benefit, general account.
Variable life: fixed premium, no guaranteed cash value, owner carries risk, separate account, needs securities license.
Variable universal life: flexible premium, no guaranteed cash value, owner carries risk, separate account, needs securities license.

## Common exam traps
- "Variable life has a flexible premium." False. Variable life is fixed. Universal is the flexible one. Variable UNIVERSAL is flexible.
- "Variable life cash value is guaranteed." False. The owner carries the investment risk.
- "Variable products are held in the general account." False. Separate account.
- "A life license alone is enough to sell variable life." False. A securities registration is also required.
- "UL Option B gives a level death benefit." False. Option B increases (face plus cash value).

## Quick check
1. Which type lets the owner change the premium amount? (Universal life)
2. Which account holds variable life money? (Separate account)
3. In whole life, who carries the investment risk? (The insurance company)
